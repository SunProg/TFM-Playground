# Full-backbone fine-tuning under multiregime contexts

## Provenance

This is a local recovery and analysis of CREATE Slurm job `36553362`, which
completed successfully on 2026-08-15 (exit code `0`). The original output
directory is no longer present on CREATE, but its complete stdout was copied
verbatim to [`evaluation.log`](evaluation.log). It evaluated the untouched
`nanotabpfn.pth` baseline alongside three full-backbone fine-tunes
(`seed2402`, `seed2403`, and `seed2404`) trained in job `36552366`.

The evaluation used support size 128, 32 queries, and 20 episodes per cell. It
tested contamination 0.0, 0.2, and 0.4 on two TabICL/SCM sources (`mlp_scm`,
`tree_scm`) and five analytic pairs. `base AUC` scores rows generated by the
dominant/base regime; `other AUC` scores rows whose labels came from the
contaminating regime. The latter is the relevant measure of multiregime
robustness.

The SCM source names need a precise reading: `mlp_scm` is a mixture of **two
separately instantiated MLP-SCM label functions**, not a mixture of an MLP-SCM
and a different named family. Likewise, `tree_scm` mixes two Tree-SCM label
functions. The two functions share the sampled SCM configuration and one
feature table, but each model is instantiated under a separate random seed;
they therefore give distinct labels for the same rows. Contamination replaces
the labels of the selected support and query rows with labels from the second
instance. These are genuine latent-regime changes, but are *within-family*
task shifts and are not directly comparable to the cross-family analytic
pairs.

## Overall results

No overall AUC was saved or printed. It cannot be recovered by averaging the
base and other AUCs: AUC depends on the ordering of predicted scores across
*both* groups, including base-versus-other positive/negative pairs. Recovering
it requires the per-query predictions and labels (or an evaluator rerun that
concatenates those rows before computing AUC). The original evaluator saved
only per-group summaries, and its output directory is no longer available;
the recovered log likewise has only the base and other summaries.

## Analysis

Across the 14 contaminated source/contamination cells, the mean change in
other-regime AUC is **-0.002**: effectively no overall improvement. Seven cells
improve and seven degrade. The three fine-tuned checkpoints beat the baseline
in 20 of the 42 individual seed-cell comparisons, so the apparent balance is
not driven by a single bad seed.

The average base-regime AUC increases by **+0.018**, while other-regime AUC is
flat. Thus fine-tuning tends to preserve or improve the dominant-regime task,
but does not reliably make the model represent both regimes at once.

There is meaningful generator dependence. On the four within-family SCM cells
the average other-regime change is **+0.046**, whereas across the ten
cross-family analytic-pair cells it is **-0.021**. This does *not* show that
fine-tuning solves cross-family mixing for SCMs: the two categories use
different constructions, and the SCM mixtures are within-family shifts. The
strongest consistent gains are `tree_scm` (+0.056 and +0.070) and
`threshold+smooth` at 20% contamination (+0.077). The strongest consistent
regressions are `sparse_interaction+dense_interaction` at 20% (-0.129) and
`linear+dense_interaction` at 20% (-0.114), where none of the three seeds
outperforms the baseline.

This is only a three-seed, 20-episode-per-cell evaluation. The copied log
includes bootstrap confidence intervals for individual cells but not a paired
cross-seed statistical test, so these differences should be treated as
directional. The supported conclusion is: **this curriculum is not a general
solution to multiregime contexts; it trades robustness among prior families
rather than delivering a consistent improvement.**
